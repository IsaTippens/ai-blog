## I. MAINSTREAM TECH & INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

### 1. Microsoft Cuts 4,800 Jobs as Capital Shifts Heavy to AI Infrastructure
In a major corporate restructuring, [Microsoft has announced it is cutting about 2.1% of its workforce](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/07/07/876369.htm), which amounts to roughly 4,800 jobs. The layoffs represent the latest entry in a persistent wave of tech sector job cuts as the Redmond giant reallocates massive amounts of capital toward multi-billion-dollar physical AI infrastructure. Notably, [Microsoft's Xbox division was disproportionately hit by the cuts](https://techweez.com/2026/07/07/microsoft-layoffs-2026-xbox-job-cuts/), losing approximately 20% of its operational staff.

### 2. Hollywood Studios Sue Midjourney over Systematic AI Copyright Infringement
In what is shaping up to be a historic legal showdown for creative intellectual property, major Hollywood entertainment giants—including [Disney, Universal, and Warner Bros.—have filed a joint lawsuit against generative art platform Midjourney](https://techweez.com/2026/07/07/microsoft-layoffs-2026-xbox-job-cuts/). The suit claims systematic copyright infringement, alleging that Midjourney trained its image-generation models on millions of copyrighted film frames, promotional materials, and character designs without securing proper licensing or compensation.

## II. COGNITIVE GOVERNANCE & REGULATION

### 3. UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance Convenes in Geneva
The first official session of the [UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance and the AI for Good Global Summit](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/07/1167862) has formally opened in Geneva. Addressing delegates, UN Secretary-General António Guterres issued an urgent call for unified global governance frameworks on advanced cognitive models and autonomous weapons ("killer robots"). Guterres warned that the rapid, runaway pace of enterprise AI adoption is significantly [outpacing both scientific understanding and legislative adaptability](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/07/1167873/), risking catastrophic systemic harm if left unchecked.

### 4. UK FCA Launches Findings and Mandates of the Mills Review on Financial AI
The UK's Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) has officially released the final findings of the long-awaited [Mills Review on AI in Financial Services](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/ai-in-financial-services-emerging-regulatory-expectations-for-governance-and-ope-102n7w7). The review establishes concrete, emerging regulatory expectations for AI governance, fair competition, algorithmic transparency, and operational resilience across the financial sector, providing a standardized playbook for how firms must manage automated decision-making and consumer protection.

### 5. China's AI Companion Law Forces Heavyweight Social Chatbot Shutdowns
China's strict new "AI Companion Law" has triggered a sudden wave of shutdowns across major regional model laboratories. Under intense governmental oversight, tech giants have been forced to [shut down personalized, custom social companion bots](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/ai-news-today-july-6-2026) on popular platforms like ByteDance's Doubao and Alibaba's Qwen, highlighting growing state scrutiny over the social and psychological implications of synthetic human-machine relationships.
