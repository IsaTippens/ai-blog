### 1. Quantum Threat to Bitcoin Cut 20x in Breakthrough 2026 Research
In a major development for decentralized security, [newly published 2026 research](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/quantum-computing-threat-to-bitcoin-2026-research-cuts-resource-gap-by-20x) has cut the estimated physical qubit requirement to crack Bitcoin's elliptic curve cryptography (ECDSA) by **20 times**. This mathematical and physical optimization shortens the theoretical timeline for quantum-safe disruption from decades to possibly within a few years (as early as 2029). In response, core developers have formally initiated defense protocols under **BIP-360** (Bitcoin Improvement Proposal 360), proposing quantum-resistant address schemes. Over $700 billion in legacy wallets remains exposed to "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks, though modern address use significantly lowers real-world vulnerability.

## III. CROSS-BORDER DATA FLOWS & FINTECH

### 5. East African Community (EAC) Finalizes Cross-Border Data Flow Framework
In a major step toward regional digital integration, the East African Community (EAC) has finalized its [proposed Cross-Border Data Flows Framework](https://techafricanews.com/2026/07/03/eac-moves-to-harmonise-cross-border-data-flows-to-advance-regional-digital-economy/) during a validation workshop in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania. Supported by the World Bank under the Eastern Africa Regional Digital Integration Project (EARDIP), the framework establishes common standards for secure, lawful, and accountable data movement across EAC partner states, promoting interoperability among national data protection systems while protecting digital trade.

### 6. Rwanda's Central Bank Deploys Proactive Digital Economy Framework
Establishing its lead in the regional digital economy, [Rwanda's central bank](https://thefintechtimes.com/rwanda-and-fintech-building-a-digital-economy-in-2026/) has deployed proactive, structured policies designed to encourage responsible fintech innovation. The central bank's framework streamlines cross-border mobile-money interoperability, micro-lending, and automated remittance clearings, making the nation a primary operational hub for East African fintech startups.

### 7. Central Bank of Sudan Orders Strict Vetting for Fintech Partnerships
Citing critical concerns regarding financial data sovereignty and unauthorized foreign influence, the [Central Bank of Sudan](https://sudantribune.com/article/315811) has issued an urgent directive. The order prohibits all domestic banks, licensed fintech firms, and financial switch operators from executing technical linkages or entering into cross-border data partnerships without securing prior central bank approval and undergo strict vetting.
