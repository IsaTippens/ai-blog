## I. SOVEREIGN AI & NATIONAL READINESS

### 1. ADB Designates Philippines for Technical AI Readiness Assistance
In a major milestone for public-sector technology integration, the [Asian Development Bank (ADB) has officially designated the Philippines](https://bworldonline.com/economy/2026/07/05/761168/adb-designates-philippines-as-potential-recipient-of-technical-assistance-to-boost-ai-readiness/) as a primary recipient of specialized technical assistance to boost national AI readiness. The program focuses on establishing regulatory sandbox guidelines, building secure government cloud infrastructure, and training civil servants on deploying automated, privacy-safe machine learning models across public utilities and social service networks.

### 2. UN Global Dialogue Prepares for Inclusive AI Governance
Ahead of its official opening in Paris, the [UN-backed Global Dialogue on Safe and Inclusive AI](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/un-global-dialogue-opens-urgent-call-safe-and-inclusive-ai-benefits-all) has released its preliminary agenda. Directed by UNESCO, the framework urges member states to co-develop unified, cross-border regulations to mitigate the widening digital and cognitive divide between developed hyper-scalers and developing nations, establishing baseline guardrails for ethical data harvesting.

## II. REGIONAL FINTECH & DATA VETTING

### 3. Central Bank of Sudan Orders Urgent Vetting for Fintech Partnerships
Citing critical concerns over financial data sovereignty and unauthorized foreign influence, the [Central Bank of Sudan has issued an urgent regulatory directive](https://sudantribune.com/article/315811). The order prohibits all licensed commercial banks, fintech startups, and electronic payment switch operators from establishing cross-border data links or executing technical partnerships without securing prior central bank approval. The crackdown follows intense local scrutiny over the launch of Al-Asjad's new digital platform, prompting regulators to enforce strict compliance and capability audits on all third-party systems.

### 4. East African Community (EAC) Concludes Dar es Salaam Workshop
Enabling a more unified digital market across East Africa, the East African Community (EAC) has successfully concluded its five-day validation workshop in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania. Supported by the World Bank under the Eastern Africa Regional Digital Integration Project (EARDIP), the newly finalized [proposed Cross-Border Data Flows Framework](https://techafricanews.com/2026/07/03/eac-moves-to-harmonise-cross-border-data-flows-to-advance-regional-digital-economy/) establishes unified security, privacy, and regulatory interoperability standards to protect consumer data during regional trade.
